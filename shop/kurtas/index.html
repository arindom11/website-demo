Journal

Why a shirt takes fourteen days

A pit loom moves at the speed of one person's hands and feet. The weaver sits with their legs below floor level, works the treadles, and throws the shuttle across a warp that was measured, starched and mounted by hand over the three days before weaving began.

Four and a half metres of 60s-count handspun cotton — enough for one shirt with the selvedge intact — comes off that loom in about fourteen days. A powerloom does the same length in under an hour. That gap is the whole of the price difference, and most of the reason this cloth exists at all.

The difference shows up in the cloth. Handloom cotton carries a slight irregularity across the weft, where the beat of the reed varied by a fraction from one pass to the next. Hold a mill shirt and a handloom shirt to a window and the mill cloth is the one with no variation in it at all.

That irregularity is not a defect and it is not a finish applied afterwards. It is the record of a person keeping time with their own body for two weeks. Cloth woven this way also wears differently: the yarn is spun with less twist and sits softer against skin from the first wash rather than the tenth.

We photograph it up close on every product page for that reason. If you are paying for handwork, you should be able to see the evidence of the hand before you buy, not after.

How it's made

Yarn, loom, finish

Step 01

Yarn

Cotton is handspun to 60s count, then sized with rice starch and dried in the sun. Colour goes in at this stage, in natural indigo and madder, before a single thread is mounted.

Step 02

Loom

The warp is measured and mounted over three days. Weaving is done on a pit loom — no power, no automation. A shirt length of 4.2 metres takes about fourteen days.

Step 03

Finish

Cloth is washed, sun-dried and cut with the selvedge kept intact. Seams, buttonholes and hems are stitched and finished by hand, then checked against the loom record.

When cloth comes off the loom

One email when a new weave is ready, and nothing else. Usually twice a month.
